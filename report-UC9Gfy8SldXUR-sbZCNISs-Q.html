This video naturally follows Mark Rober's storytelling playbook — the same framework Anthony studied with Aarron during coaching:
Question "What happens when an aerobatic plane flies formation with a Vietnam-era military helicopter?" The title itself poses the question. Viewers click to find out.
Journey Aarron builds real tension: a surprise change of plans (Cleetus arrives with his own helicopter), the team pulling out the Huey, a safety brief about "the big blender," then the synchronized grass-strip takeoff. No framework applied ❌
35 minutes of raw cockpit footage with no narration, no captions, no story structure, no character voice. None of the frameworks apply because there's no story being told:
No Question The title is a label, not a hook. "Cockpit View Extra 330 Aerobatics" tells you what it is, not why you should care. There's no curiosity gap to pull viewers in.
No Tension Without narration, there are no stakes. Viewers don't know what's difficult, what's dangerous, or what to look for. Even spectacular footage lacks the emotional scaffolding that keeps people watching past minute one.
No Payoff At 35:33 with no narrative arc, there's no reason for YouTube to recommend it and no reason for viewers to finish it. The algorithm can't promote what viewers don't watch.
The lesson: Same pilot, same aircraft, same week — 1,560× difference in views. The footage isn't the problem. The absence of storytelling is. This video is B-roll waiting for a narrative to be built around it.
