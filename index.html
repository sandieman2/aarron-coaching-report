Storytelling Analysis

📖
Top vs. bottom performing video
Evaluated against vidIQ storytelling frameworks: emotion, tension, and character transformation
🔥 Top Performer
I Flew Next to a Real Military Helicopter ft Cleetus McFarland
Apr 2 · 11:42 · 57,763 views
Framework match: "Question → Journey → Discovery" ✅
This video naturally follows Mark Rober's storytelling playbook — the same framework Anthony studied with Aarron during coaching:
Question "What happens when an aerobatic plane flies formation with a Vietnam-era military helicopter?" The title itself poses the question. Viewers click to find out.
Journey Aarron builds real tension: a surprise change of plans (Cleetus arrives with his own helicopter), the team pulling out the Huey, a safety brief about "the big blender," then the synchronized grass-strip takeoff. Each beat raises stakes. His narration adds a character arc — a world-class pilot whose genuine excitement (and nerves) make him relatable.
Discovery The payoff: formation flight with smoke at 100 knots, tight turns next to the Huey rotors. Emotional resolution — Aarron's reaction sells the moment. Viewers feel like they were inside something they'll never get to do.
Why it worked: The Cleetus collab brought the audience, but the storytelling kept them watching. Clear protagonist (Aarron), rising tension (will the formation work?), surprising twist (Cleetus arriving), emotional payoff. At 11:42, pacing was tight — no dead air.
⚠️ Underperformer
Cockpit View Extra 330 Aerobatics | Santa Maria Airshow POV
Apr 8 · 35:33 · 37 views
No framework applied ❌
35 minutes of raw cockpit footage with no narration, no captions, no story structure, no character voice. None of the frameworks apply because there's no story being told:
No Question The title is a label, not a hook. "Cockpit View Extra 330 Aerobatics" tells you what it is, not why you should care. There's no curiosity gap to pull viewers in.
No Tension Without narration, there are no stakes. Viewers don't know what's difficult, what's dangerous, or what to look for. Even spectacular footage lacks the emotional scaffolding that keeps people watching past minute one.
No Payoff At 35:33 with no narrative arc, there's no reason for YouTube to recommend it and no reason for viewers to finish it. The algorithm can't promote what viewers don't watch.
The lesson: Same pilot, same aircraft, same week — 1,560× difference in views. The footage isn't the problem. The absence of storytelling is. This video is B-roll waiting for a narrative to be built around it.

Best for tutorials & how-to. Could work for "How I prepare for an airshow" or "Fixing my plane after a rough landing" — not Aarron's primary format.
🟡 Occasional use
Question → Journey → Discovery ✅
Aarron's strongest framework. Every airshow is a journey. Every formation flight is a question. His best video already follows this naturally — now make it intentional on every video.
🟢 Primary framework

Perfect for milestone content: "How I went from student pilot to world champion" or "My first airshow vs now." Use 1–2x per quarter for personal brand-building.
