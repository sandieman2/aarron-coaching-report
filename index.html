🎬 You're implementing fast
After we studied Mark Rober's hook structure together on April 14 ("These 500 drones, I programmed with one vision in mind…"), you came back two days later with: "Our next video has that heads up in the Intro hook. I start by saying this airshow is all about the race, the vendetta I have against a jet truck." That's the exact narrative framing we discussed. You made edits, asked for a green light, and posted within the week. Review → implement → publish is the cycle that accelerates growth, and you're already there.
📺 Your TV audience changes the game
A standout finding from our analytics review: 46% of your viewers watch on TV, vs 25% mobile. As I told you: "This means you are at the level of Mr. Beast, Ryan Trahan. Your production always has to be incredible." Most creators skew mobile-first. Your TV-heavy audience means every frame of your airshow footage is being seen on big screens — which is a massive advantage if production quality stays high. It also explains why the raw 35-min cockpit POV underperformed.
🖼️ Thumbnails: craft for strangers, not fans
From my April 20 review: "The thumbnail didn't do the video justice. I see Corvette and then I see you in a cockpit, but we always have to craft thumbnails not for your current subscribers who know what you're doing — we need to craft thumbnails and titles around people who have no idea." This is the #1 lever for your long-form. Your Shorts already hook with curiosity ("Can you guess what the guy with the light gun is doing?"). Apply that same stranger-first thinking to every long-form thumbnail.
✈️ Production quality is your moat
From my very first review: "I took a look at your channel and it is effing cool. Something that a lot of people can't replicate, it's super unique." That's still true. The video quality and the production is there. Nobody else on YouTube is shooting from the cockpit of an Extra 330SC at airshows. Your job isn't to compete with other creators on editing tricks — it's to frame your one-of-a-kind footage with hooks, stories, and thumbnails that make strangers click. The footage does the rest.
